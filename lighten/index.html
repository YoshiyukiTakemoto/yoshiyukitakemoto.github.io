Compress and resize images without uploading

Drop in photos to make them smaller, resize them and convert to WebP, JPEG, PNG or AVIF. Everything happens on your device, so it’s fast and private.

    How to use

    1. Pick an output format. WebP gives the best size for most photos; JPEG works everywhere.
    2. Set the quality (80 is a good start) and, if you like, a maximum size for the longest side.
    3. Drop images on the box, choose them, or paste with Ctrl+V / ⌘V.
    4. Download each image, or all of them at once as a ZIP file.

    Which format should I use?

    • WebP: 25–35% smaller than JPEG at the same quality. Supported by all current browsers.
    • AVIF: often smaller still, but slower to create. Appears in the list only if your browser can make it.
    • JPEG: the safest choice for email, older software and printing services.
    • PNG: lossless. Use it for screenshots, diagrams and images that need transparency.

    Common sizes

    For a website, 1920 px on the longest side covers full-screen images on most displays. For email attachments and chat apps, 1280 px is usually plenty. Social networks resize uploads themselves, so 2048 px is a safe upper limit.

    Frequently asked questions

    Are my images uploaded?

    No. Your browser decodes and re-encodes each image locally. You can disconnect from the internet after the page loads and it still works.

    Is there a file size or count limit?

    No fixed limit. Very large batches depend on your device’s memory.

    Does it remove location data?

    Yes. Re-encoding removes EXIF metadata such as GPS location, camera model and serial numbers. The image is rotated to the correct orientation first.

    Why is a file sometimes kept as the original?

    When the image is already well compressed and you keep the same format and size, re-encoding can make it larger. In that case the original file is kept.
