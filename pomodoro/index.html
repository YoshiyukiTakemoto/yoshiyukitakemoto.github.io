Pomodoro timer

Work in focused 25-minute sessions with short breaks in between. The countdown shows in your browser tab, and the timer keeps going even if you reload the page.

25:00

How to use

  1. Press Start (or the space bar) to begin a 25-minute focus session.
  2. When the time is up you hear a sound and the break starts automatically.
  3. After every four focus sessions you get a longer break.
  4. Change the lengths in Settings. Your settings and session history stay in this browser.

What is the Pomodoro Technique?

A time management method created by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s. You pick one task, work on it for 25 minutes without interruption, then take a 5-minute break. Each 25-minute block is one “pomodoro”, named after the tomato-shaped kitchen timer he used. After four pomodoros you take a longer break of 15 to 30 minutes.

Tips

  • Write down the one task for the session before you press Start.
  • If something else comes to mind, note it and return to the task.
  • Step away from the screen during breaks.
  • Add this page to your home screen to open it like an app.

Frequently asked questions

Does the timer keep running in a background tab?

Yes. The timer is based on the end time, so it stays accurate even when the browser slows down background tabs. The tab title shows the time left.

Can I change 25 and 5 minutes?

Yes. Some people prefer 50/10 or 90/20. Set any length from 1 to 180 minutes.

Why don’t I hear a sound?

Browsers only allow sound after you interact with the page, so press Start yourself at least once. Also check that the tab isn’t muted.
